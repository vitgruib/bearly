Luma
Site
luma.com
Checked
Every 3 hours
How
GET requests to Luma's JSON API at api.lu.ma: /discover/get-paginated-events for events near campus, /calendar/get-items for each followed calendar, and /user/profile/events-hosting for each organizer.
Gets
Title, time, venue and map location, host, description, cover image, free or paid.
Found by
Three ways:
  1. Luma's discover feed: the events nearest campus, plus its AI, Tech, Climate and Arts feeds.
  2. 48 calendars and 17 organizer profiles followed by name: campus clubs and programs, and Bay Area tech, startup and hackathon groups. The list is in sources.toml.
  3. Automatically: once an organization's calendar has two events within 3 km of campus in the discover feed, all of its events are followed. It's dropped after 120 days with no events near campus.

How events are tagged

Every event goes through the same fixed rules, whatever the source. The rules are deterministic: the same text always gets the same tags, and no person or AI checks them. So treat tags, locations, "Free" and "Getting in" as a helpful guess, not 100% ground truth: an event can be missing a tag it deserves or carry one it doesn't. The event's own page is the final word.

Location

The first rule that fits decides:

  1. Marked online, with no campus room named: Online. A hybrid event in a campus room is Campus.
  2. A concert at one of the big venues above, outside the Bay Area: Sacramento, Los Angeles or San Diego. These are the only events listed that far away.
  3. Campus means on UC Berkeley's own land. That's its map coordinates inside the university's boundary as OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) draws it (the main campus and hill, Clark Kerr, the residence halls, BAMPFA, Berkeley Way West, ...). Or, for a listing with no coordinates, a venue that names a campus building ("241 Giannini Hall", "Wheeler 124", "MLK Student Union"), a Haas room on the campus calendar ("C250"), or UC Berkeley's own ZIP code, 94720. Downtown and Southside are Berkeley, however close: The Freight, Shattuck Ave and Telegraph Ave are not on campus.
  4. Has map coordinates: within 4 km of campus, or the city is Berkeley, is Berkeley. More than 110 km away is not listed.
  5. Has a city (the listing's own city field, matched exactly, ignoring case):
    • Berkeley is Berkeley.
    • These 25 are East Bay: Alameda, Albany, Castro Valley, Concord, Danville, Dublin, El Cerrito, El Sobrante, Emeryville, Fremont, Hayward, Kensington, Lafayette, Livermore, Moraga, Oakland, Orinda, Piedmont, Pleasant Hill, Pleasanton, Richmond, San Leandro, San Ramon, Union City and Walnut Creek.
    • San Francisco (or "SF") is San Francisco.
    • These 22 are Peninsula & South Bay: Belmont, Burlingame, Cupertino, Daly City, Foster City, Half Moon Bay, Hillsborough, Los Altos, Menlo Park, Millbrae, Milpitas, Mountain View, Palo Alto, Redwood City, San Bruno, San Carlos, San Jose, San Mateo, Santa Clara, South San Francisco, Stanford and Sunnyvale.
    • Any other city falls through to the next rules, so an event in, say, Marin or Vallejo is placed by its coordinates.
  6. Coordinates but an unknown city: west of longitude −122.36 between latitudes 37.70 and 37.82 is San Francisco; north of 37.95 is North Bay; south of 37.70 is Peninsula & South Bay; anything else is East Bay.
  7. No location at all: Berkeley if it came from a club or campus calendar, a mailing list or Instagram, since those are local. From anywhere else it is not listed.

Auto tagging

The title, description, host and any category the source gives (such as the campus calendar's event type) are searched for the keywords below, ignoring case. An event gets every tag with a match, so it can have several or none. A keyword also matches longer words ("recruit" matches "recruiting"), except short ones like AI, DJ or run, which must be whole words.

Free

Listed as free or $0 by the source, or the description says free admission, free entry, free event, free to attend or no cost. Free only shows these.

Getting in

Each card says how to get in when the source or the description tells us. Luma and Partiful say whether to RSVP, whether the host approves guests, whether it's sold out or has a waitlist, and the ticket price; the campus calendar gives the cost. The title and description are also read for: RSVP required, registration required, open to everyone, no RSVP needed, Berkeley students only, members only, invite only, 21+, 18+, bring your Cal ID, and first come, first served. When a platform takes RSVPs, wording like "drop in" doesn't override it. Nothing listed means nobody said, so check the event's page.

Subscribe

📅 Subscribe on the events page gives a calendar link for everything or for one event type, such as 🍕 Free food. Add it once and new events appear in Google, Apple or Outlook Calendar by themselves. The feeds are rebuilt every 3 hours; each calendar app checks for changes on its own schedule (Apple can check every few minutes, Google every several hours). Each feed is a plain file in data/cal/, like free-food.ics.

Source

Where the event was found. The same event found in several places is merged into one, and the card shows every source.

Open data and code

Lo-Cal is transparent and scrapable. Every event on the site is in one public file, data/events.json, rebuilt every 3 hours. It needs no key and no login, so read it, scrape it, mirror it or build on it. Each source's health is in data/state.json. Every scraper and every rule on this page is open source on GitHub, and events are sorted by time only.

For AI assistants: llms.txt explains the data and the URL filters. data/llms/ splits upcoming events into small plain-text files (today, tomorrow, this weekend, each day, each topic) that a chatbot can read in one fetch, so you can point your own assistant at Lo-Cal and ask "what's on at Berkeley tonight?" and get real listings. llms-full.txt has every event in one file. Each event also has its own page, e/<id>.html, listed in sitemap.xml.
