# Precedents: campus event discovery products at US universities other than Berkeley (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: about 22 searches and fetches. Coverage is strongest on institutional platforms (Corq/Engage, Presence, CampusGroups), the big Gen Z social apps (Fizz, Yik Yak/Sidechat, Partiful, Facebook Campus) and a handful of recent student-built aggregators. Several names in the brief (Unation, Atlas, Hivemind, Clubhub, a Luma "campus push") returned no usable sources and are listed under Gaps rather than filled in from memory.

## Startups and commercial platforms: funding, traction, shutdowns, pivots

### Takeaway
The institution-sold "student engagement" platforms (Corq/Engage, Presence, CampusGroups) survived by selling to student-affairs offices and were then rolled up by PE-backed consolidators. Anthology, which owned Engage and Corq, went through Chapter 11 in 2025. Student-facing social apps with campus wedges either died (Yik Yak 2017, Facebook Campus 2022, Wigo) or grew by becoming general social, marketplace or commerce apps (Fizz, Sidechat). None of the venture-scale winners is primarily an event-discovery product. Partiful grew on event *hosting* and pays campus ambassadors to recruit hosts.

### Cited Findings
**Corq / Campus Labs / Anthology Engage (institutional)**
- Corq is a free mobile events app published by Campus Labs and "powered by Campus Labs Engage". Many schools still pointed students to it in 2024-2026 (Purdue, KU, Dickinson, Bucknell, Coastal Carolina, St. Francis). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/corq/id940682997); [Purdue](https://purdue.edu/sao/resources/CORQ%20App.html); [KU](https://technology.ku.edu/catalog/corq-mobile-app); [Dickinson](https://www.dickinson.edu/corq); [Bucknellian](https://bucknellian.net/?p=50857)
- Campus Labs bought OrgSync (Mar 2016), CoursEval (2017) and Chalk & Wire (2018). In July 2020 it merged with Campus Management and iModules to form Anthology. — [Mergr](https://mergr.com/transaction/anthology-acquires-campus-labs); [Campus Technology, 2020-07-07](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2020/07/07/campus-management-campus-labs-imodules-merge-to-form-new-company.aspx?admgarea=news)
- Anthology filed Chapter 11 on 2025-09-29. On 2025-11-11 the court approved selling the "Lifecycle Engagement" unit, which includes Engage, to Encoura. Anthology emerged in March 2026 rebranded as Blackboard. Reported closing dates conflict (2025-12-31 or 2026-01-31). — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/index%2ephp/experience/anthology-chapter-11-restructuring); [BusinessWire 2025-11-24](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251124252849/en); [BusinessWire 2026-01-23](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260123909985/en); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2414459)
- Saginaw Valley State (MI) used Corq as its default event calendar for 6 years, the last 3 with the app. An associate dean said "the company that supported Corq went out of business", which ended the contract early. Its replacement, a Pathify-built calendar inside the MySVSU portal, sits behind MFA and hides closed groups such as Greek life. RSO leaders report turnout down by about half at some events and say "no one" they know uses the new calendar. Students mainly use Instagram, a weekly "Event Blast" email (about 60% open rate), flyers and RSO lists. — [Valley Vanguard, 2026-03-20](https://thevalleyvanguard.com/2026/03/20/after-corq-rsos-struggle-to-promote-events)
- Competitor Ready Education (owner of CampusGroups) says more than 100 former Anthology Engage institutions have moved to CampusGroups. It also markets that Engage's "future [is] uncertain after bankruptcy and divestment". This is vendor marketing and unverified. — [Ready Education](https://www.readyeducation.com/articles/replacing-anthology-engage-100-institutions-and-counting); [Ready Ed 2-pager](https://resources.readyeducation.com/hubfs/Engage%202%20pager%20Logos.pdf?hsLang=en)

**CampusGroups / Ready Education**
- Ready Education, backed by Level Equity, acquired CampusGroups (announced 2022-05-16). — [Ready Education blog](https://www.readyeducation.com/blog/new-partnership-ready-education-acquires-campusgroups); [EDUCAUSE](https://www.educause.edu/about/corporate-participation/member-press-releases/ready-education-acquires-campusgroups)

**Presence**
- Presence is a campus engagement and involvement-data platform founded by Reuben Pressman in St. Petersburg, FL. Its founding year is given as 2012 or 2014 depending on the source. Modern Campus (Riverside Company) acquired it on about 2021-06-24, when it had 250+ institutional customers. Terms were undisclosed. — [Modern Campus](https://moderncampus.com/about/newsroom/modern-campus-acquires-presence.html); [St Pete Catalyst](https://stpetecatalyst.com/edtech-startup-presence-acquired-by-larger-firm/); [PE Hub](https://pehub.com/riverside-backed-modern-campus-acquires-presence)

**Fizz (Stanford-founded, .edu-verified social app)**
- Total raised is about $41M to $41.5M: a $12M Series A plus a $25M Series B led by Owl Ventures with NEA. — [The Star, 2024-07](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/07/22/this-gen-z-startup-raised-us415mil-as-the-anti-facebook); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/fizz-25-million-funding/)
- As of 2025-09 Fizz was on more than 620 US campuses, had added 250+ in one month, had quadrupled daily active users in that month, and had 27M posts to date. It expanded into a marketplace and Gopuff-powered grocery delivery ("Fizz Store"). The article does not mention an events feature. — [TechCrunch, 2025-09-03](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/03/college-social-app-fizz-expands-into-grocery-delivery)
- An unrelated fintech company is also called Fizz (student debit card). Do not conflate the two. — [Fizz fintech factsheet](https://llms.joinfizz.com/fizz-company-product-factsheet)

**Yik Yak / Sidechat**
- Yik Yak shut down on 2017-05-05 amid declining use and cyberbullying and threat controversies, relaunched in Aug 2021 (about 3.5M installs since), and was bought by rival Sidechat in Mar 2023. Users protested that it became "Sidechat reskinned" and required .edu emails. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yik_Yak); [TechCrunch, 2023-04-03](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/03/yik-yak-users-are-protesting-the-apps-makeover-after-acquisition-by-sidechat/)

**Facebook Campus**
- Launched fall 2020 as a .edu-only section of Facebook with groups, campus events and admin updates, and grew from 60 to 204 schools. Facebook ended it on 2022-03-10, saying Groups worked better. Few users commented on the closure, which suggests little traction. — [TechCrunch, 2022-03-02](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/02/facebook-is-shutting-down-its-college-student-only-social-network-campus/amp)

**Wigo (college party/nightlife app)**
- Reportedly raised about $14M in 13 months, then dropped its app after merging with faster-growing rival Yeti. Details are thin and the source is an aggregator. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/wigo-inc)

**Partiful**
- Founded in 2020 by Joy Tao and Shreya Murthy. Raised a $20M Series A led by a16z in late 2022 (about $27M total) and says it has not needed to raise since. Named Google's Best App of 2024. — [Fortune, 2023-05-23](https://fortune.com/2023/05/23/partiful-founders-startup-raises-series-a/); [TechCrunch, 2024-11-18](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/partiful-is-googles-best-app-of-2024/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partiful)
- Partiful runs a paid part-time campus growth program for spring 2026, extended to the UK and Spain. "Campus Growth Managers" launch it on campus in an "intensive activation week" and recruit students, student orgs and venues as *hosts*. — [Talentify job post](https://www.talentify.io/job/campus-growth-manager-spring-2026-myrtle-point-oregon-us-partiful-af908f89-626d-4d26-a851-a39ba62737ff); [Hirebase UK/Spain](https://beta.hirebase.org/company/partiful/jobs/campus-growth-ambassador-uk-and-spain-spring-2026-a2ceb9ea)

**Luma**
- Clubs routinely publish whole calendars on Luma (for example FIU's Code Crunch clubs). I found no official Luma campus program or campus metrics. Funding data is muddled by name collisions: a $3M seed led by Maven and Venrock is the only clean data point. — [lu.ma CodeStorm listing](https://lu.ma/j1w3bo9m); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/?p=39471)

### Inferences
- The durable money in this category comes from selling to administrators (compliance, involvement tracking, co-curricular data), not from discovery for students. Even that segment is fragile: the Corq/Engage line went through a bankruptcy sale, and SVSU shows schools can lose their only centralized feed overnight. A free, scraper-based layer that does not depend on any one vendor contract is a hedge against that churn.
- Venture-backed student social apps that reached scale treated campus as a wedge for anonymous chat, a marketplace or commerce, never for event listings. That points to event discovery alone being too small or too seasonal to monetize at venture scale. It fits a free or open-source framing.
- Partiful and Luma win supply by making *hosting* better (RSVPs, guest lists), so discovery is a byproduct. An aggregator can ride on that supply, since Luma and Partiful pages are public, instead of competing with it.
- The SVSU case is a natural experiment: removing a single-pane calendar measurably hurt turnout. Even so, the channels students actually used were Instagram and a curated weekly email (60% open rate). The demand is for a curated push channel, not a portal behind a login.

### Gaps
- Unation, "Atlas", Hivemind, Clubhub/"Gather"-type campus apps: no reliable sources found. They may be obscure, renamed, or names that collide with unrelated companies.
- Luma's campus strategy and campus usage numbers: no first-party data found.
- Partiful's college share of users and the results of its ambassador program: not public.
- Corq's current status after the Engage sale to Encoura is unconfirmed (the App Store listing still exists). Whether SVSU's "went out of business" refers to Anthology's Chapter 11 is my inference.
- Did not research Localist/Concept3D, iCommunify, CampVents or Eventum beyond confirming they exist as vendors. — [Concept3D](https://concept3d.com/student-event-calendar-software/); [CampVents](https://campvents.app/)

## Student-built aggregators at other schools: what got usage, how, and why they died

### Takeaway
The most durable student-run "discovery" channels are dumb and push-based: free-food mailing lists at MIT, Stanford and Princeton have run for over a decade on volunteer moderation and crowd posting. Recent student-built aggregators (UIUC 2026, Claremont 2024, Swarthmore 2023, UT Austin in development) get early traction through Instagram scraping or a viral launch post, then hit maintenance and scraping-fragility walls. I found no well-documented postmortem of a student aggregator dying.

### Cited Findings
**Free-food lists (long-lived, push, crowd-sourced)**
- MIT's free-food list is "known far & wide". After spammers infiltrated it, it was moved to free-foods@mit.edu with moderation; mit.edu senders post immediately and others are moderated. The Tech (2011) called free food "practically an MIT subculture" with multiple lists. An MIT 2017 guide said the list is "hit or miss". — [MIT mailman](https://mailman.mit.edu/mailman/listinfo/free-foods); [The Tech, 2011-04-12](https://thetech.com/2011/04/12/freefood-v131-n19); [MIT OGE](https://oge.mit.edu/always-where-the-foods-at)
- Stanford's free-food list (2012) had 463 subscribers, volunteer student moderators, 2-3 alerts a week, a fixed "[location]:[description]" subject format and a companion blog archive. Volume peaks at the start of the year. — [Stanford Daily, 2012-10-12](https://stanforddaily.com/?p=1071738)
- Princeton's free-food listserv was founded in 2011 by students and later run by the student government (USG) IT committee. It carried 900+ emails in 2014-15 (about 2/day), but about a third of days had none. Fridays and Frist Campus Center peaked. The Data Science Club analyzed the archive. — [Daily Princetonian, 2015-11-09](https://www.dailyprincetonian.com/article/2015/11/listserv-analysis-shows-frist-murray-dodge-most-popular-places-for-free-food-distribution)

**Recent aggregators and apps**
- **eventsatuiuc.com (UIUC, launched 2026-04-26):** a sophomore's site that monitors 900+ RSO Instagram accounts and uses AI to classify each account's three most recent posts as events. It had to run a fake account to get around Instagram bot protection, was still updated by hand daily as of May 2026, and had 1,500+ visitors in its first ~10 days. — [Daily Illini, 2026-05-05](https://dailyillini.com/news-stories/news-features/2026/05/05/ui-cs-student-creates-events-page-screen-time-app/)
- **5C Events (Claremont Colleges, launched Oct 2024):** built by two first-years mostly in Glide (a no-code AI app builder). Launched with a post on Fizz that got "thousands of likes", and its Instagram reached 900+ followers within weeks. Stated challenges: growing users, getting clubs to self-enter events, near-daily feature requests, and balancing it with coursework. — [The Student Life, 2024-11](https://tsl.news/new-5c-events-app-available-to-students-across-the-campuses/)
- **Spotlyte (Swarthmore, launched 2023-01-19):** a host-your-own social events app from four sophomores, with 400+ downloads in its first week. It relied on user-created events only, planned to license to other colleges for revenue, and was supported by the college's innovation office. Current status unknown. — [Swarthmore Phoenix, 2023-02-02](https://swarthmorephoenix.com/2023/02/02/students-launch-mobile-app-to-spotlight-campus-social-scene%EF%BF%BC/)
- **Longhorn Loop (UT Austin, Longhorn Developers club):** an Expo/React Native app on Cloudflare Workers + D1 with scrapers. GitHub issues track per-department page scrapers with a goal of capturing at least 95% of listed events per run. As of Nov 2026 it was in beta (9 stars, 18 forks, 118 open issues). Because a student *club* runs it rather than one person, it may hold up better when maintainers graduate. — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/Longhorn-Developers/Longhorn-Loop); [Scraper issue #323](https://github.com/Longhorn-Developers/Longhorn-Loop/issues/323)
- **Others:** an HBCU "agentic campus event aggregator" ("Yard"), a UC Santa Cruz scraper that feeds events.ucsc.edu into Localist (built by the university), and many hackathon or portfolio aggregators on Devpost and GitHub with no evident users. — [Yard](https://github.com/angelic-coder19/yard); [ucsc/events-scraper](https://github.com/ucsc/events-scraper); [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/campus-event-aggregator)
- A 2016 Show HN for an event-discovery site drew comments about scraping Facebook events. That source has since been closed off by Facebook's API restrictions. — [HN 11166062](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11166062)

### Inferences
- The free-food lists have lasted 10+ years because they need almost no infrastructure (Mailman), the crowd supplies the content, and someone institutional (IT, student government) inherits moderation. They show that a low-tech push channel outlives clever apps.
- Instagram is where club supply lives in 2024-2026 (UIUC with 900+ accounts, SVSU's "80,000 clubs" remark), but scraping it means fighting bot protection and still doing manual review. That is the most likely failure point for a solo maintainer.
- Launch spikes are easy (Fizz post, Instagram, 400 downloads in a week). Retention and keeping supply current are the hard parts. Few sources follow up a year later, which is itself a signal.
- Apps that require clubs to self-submit (5C Events, Spotlyte) struggle to get clubs to adopt them, which supports a scrape-first design like Lo-Cal's.

### Gaps
- Searches turned up no aggregators for Harvard, MIT (beyond the free-food lists), CMU, Penn, Michigan, UCLA, Cornell or Dartmouth. My memory suggests some exist, such as Penn Labs' apps and Dartmouth listservs, but nothing here was verified, so I omit them.
- No documented student-aggregator shutdown postmortem found, whether from a graduated maintainer, broken scraping or low traffic. The current status of Spotlyte and 5C Events is unknown.
- No retention or usage numbers beyond launch-week figures.

## Recurring lessons: cold start, scraping vs submission, distribution, sustainability, monetization

### Takeaway
The evidence favors scraping or aggregating existing supply over asking for submissions, pushing through email or Instagram over pull-based portals, and handing ownership to an institution or club over leaving it with one student. Monetization at the student level is unproven. The only reliable payers are administrations, and that market is consolidating and churning.

### Cited Findings
- **Cold start, supply side:** submission-based apps cite getting clubs to enter events as their main challenge (5C Events). Partiful pays ambassadors to recruit hosts during an "activation week". Scrapers sidestep this by ingesting 900+ existing Instagram accounts (UIUC). — [TSL](https://tsl.news/new-5c-events-app-available-to-students-across-the-campuses/); [Partiful job](https://www.talentify.io/job/campus-growth-manager-spring-2026-myrtle-point-oregon-us-partiful-af908f89-626d-4d26-a851-a39ba62737ff); [Daily Illini](https://dailyillini.com/news-stories/news-features/2026/05/05/ui-cs-student-creates-events-page-screen-time-app/)
- **Scraping fragility:** Instagram bot protection forced a fake-account workaround and daily manual updates at UIUC. MIT's open list had to be rebuilt after spam. — [Daily Illini](https://dailyillini.com/news-stories/news-features/2026/05/05/ui-cs-student-creates-events-page-screen-time-app/); [MIT mailman](https://mailman.mit.edu/mailman/listinfo/free-foods)
- **Distribution:** a weekly curated email averaged about 60% opens at SVSU, and Instagram is the dominant channel there. A login- and MFA-gated portal calendar was ignored. — [Valley Vanguard](https://thevalleyvanguard.com/2026/03/20/after-corq-rsos-struggle-to-promote-events)
- **Launch channels:** anonymous campus apps such as Fizz work as launch megaphones (5C Events). — [TSL](https://tsl.news/new-5c-events-app-available-to-students-across-the-campuses/)
- **Seasonality:** free-food volume peaks at the start of the year and in fall and drops in summer. About a third of days at Princeton had no posts. — [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/?p=1071738); [Daily Princetonian](https://www.dailyprincetonian.com/article/2015/11/listserv-analysis-shows-frist-murray-dodge-most-popular-places-for-free-food-distribution)
- **Platform-owned campus products fail too:** Facebook Campus reached 204 schools but was closed for lack of use, with Groups doing the job better. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/02/facebook-is-shutting-down-its-college-student-only-social-network-campus/amp)
- **Monetization:** institution SaaS (Presence, CampusGroups, Engage) gets acquired by PE roll-ups. Consumer campus apps monetize through commerce such as Fizz's Gopuff delivery, not events. Spotlyte's plan to license to colleges is unverified as having produced revenue. — [Modern Campus](https://moderncampus.com/about/newsroom/modern-campus-acquires-presence.html); [TechCrunch Fizz](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/03/college-social-app-fizz-expands-into-grocery-delivery); [Swarthmore Phoenix](https://swarthmorephoenix.com/2023/02/02/students-launch-mobile-app-to-spotlight-campus-social-scene%EF%BF%BC/)

### Inferences
- **Design implications for Lo-Cal:**
  - Scrape-first, with Luma, Partiful and campus calendars (stable, public) as the backbone and Instagram as the fragile tier.
  - Push distribution: a weekly email digest, an Instagram account and iCal subscribe, rather than a portal.
  - Free food as a high-engagement hook, since it has a decade of proven demand.
  - Plan for succession from day one: a club or org owns the repo, scrapers are documented, there is a test harness per source, and there is no single-person manual daily step.
- Recruiter or sponsor monetization is plausible but undocumented in this category. Treat it as an untested hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on iCal-subscribe or Discord adoption for campus aggregators.
- No sourced examples of recruiter or sponsor revenue for student event aggregators.
- No quantitative comparison of retention for scraped versus submitted supply.
